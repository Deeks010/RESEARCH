Recommendation

Test a focused website improvement service for owner-run visitor farms: farm visits, school trips, seasonal events and direct sales. Start with one booking or visit-planning page.

Pennsylvania, North Carolina and Ohio are a first research sample with comparable visitor-farm offers and accessible local business sources. They are not ranked as America's highest-spending states. County is recorded for local grouping. Evaluate the business and issue before the state.

Why this segment: visitors must decide when to come, what it costs and how to reserve. A clearer page can be tested against those tasks. Commodity-only farms may have little reason to buy a consumer website redesign. This is a hypothesis to test, not a claim that farms are the most profitable niche.

What to sell first: a visit-planning or booking page with one clear action, verified current details and an easy seasonal update process. A full rebrand is a larger decision and is not justified by a typo or one outdated event.

Market evidence and real competing offers

US agritourism generated $1.26 billion in 2022, 12.4% above 2017 in inflation-adjusted terms. Revenue occurred in about 57% of counties. This supports an existing customer-facing market, not website agency demand or individual budgets. USDA, 2022 census data; published December 2024

The 2022 census reported 116,617 farms with $3.3 billion in direct-to-consumer sales. Direct sales and agritourism are different categories and should not be treated as identical buyers. USDA census release

Penn State recommends keeping current business information and prices on farm websites. Its agritourism pricing guidance cites an older survey with low profits and cash-flow constraints. That is a reason to qualify budgets, not assign that historical survey result to current prospects. Extension guidance; indexed text reviewed, live access failed

How to use the shortlist

Priority: stronger page-level evidence and an identifiable business offer. Start a qualification conversation; this does not mean the business needs a full redesign.

Conditional: plausible improvement but facts, business impact, owner access or timing need checking.

Reserve: functioning site, weak current need or material evidence gaps. Do not spend unpaid design time yet.

Email labels distinguish a named owner from a general business inbox. An address on the same page as an owner is not automatically owner-direct.

No visual browser surface was available. Reviews use public page text and links only; no desktop/mobile appearance, speed, accessibility, search ranking, form delivery or checkout completion is claimed tested. A fetch failure is not proof that customers see a broken site. Emails are public business contacts; guessed, masked and private addresses are excluded. No company has confirmed willingness to buy. No outreach or website redesign was performed.

Test the business before building 30 free websites

Start with five high-evidence prospects. Prepare a brief annotated concept for one customer task per business, with a strict time cap. Ask for a discussion before building a full replacement. If the first ten well-matched contacts yield no relevant problem conversations, revise the segment or offer before making more free designs. This is an experimental rule, not an expected response rate.

  1. Confirm business is operating and receives meaningful inquiries or visits.
  2. Document a specific customer task and the exact page evidence. Do not equate phone booking with a broken website.
  3. Ask whether the issue creates repeated questions, incomplete inquiries or lost bookings. Only the owner/analytics can establish actual impact.
  4. Confirm website control, existing agency/platform contract, decision-maker, budget and timing.
  5. Offer one bounded improvement and a paid scope. Current owners must confirm opening times, prices and capacity before any implementation.
Time and money: planning example

Illustration only: 10 prospects × 45 minutes research/concept = 7.5 hours. One $750 project requiring another 12 hours yields $750 / 19.5 = about $38.46 per hour before costs, support, tax and uncounted sales time. Zero sales yields zero revenue. These are chosen planning assumptions, not observed conversion or pricing data.

Seasonal timing matters: September can be peak preparation for fall attractions. Ask when they can discuss improvements; do not assume a live-season rebuild is welcome.
