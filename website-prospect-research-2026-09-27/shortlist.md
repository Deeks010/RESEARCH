## 1. J&A Lawn & Landscape — Belvidere, Illinois

[J&A Lawn & Landscape](https://www.jalawn.com/)

The clearest gap between an established business and an underdeveloped website.

**Evidence:** Angi displays 4.8/5 from 127 reviews. BBB identifies Jeff Sawallisch as owner and records a 1985 business start. The newest review visible in the reviewed Angi extract was from 2024; current booking capacity is unknown.

- [Angi profile](https://www.angi.com/companylist/us/il/belvidere/ja-lawn-landscape-reviews-1.htm/)
- [BBB profile](https://www.bbb.org/us/il/belvidere/profile/landscape-contractors/j-a-lawn-landscape-0654-88283588)

**Website observations:**

- An oversized logo dominates the opening screen. The main headline gives little reason to choose this company.
- The site still describes 30 years of experience and carries a 2015 footer. A separate gallery contains 39 slides; the opening slide is the logo.
- The homepage does not bring customer reviews or clear project examples into the sales story. Its Facebook and Twitter links point to Wix accounts. A generic enquiry form does exist on the Contact page.

**Offer:** Lead with a strong completed patio or retaining-wall project. Name the service area. Bring verified reviews and three project stories onto the homepage. Offer a prominent quote request with project type, ZIP code and preferred contact method.

**Measure:** Qualified quote enquiries → estimates booked → profitable jobs won. Agree the target service and actual coverage with the owner first.

**Public contact:** Jeff Sawallisch — owner listed by BBB. Public business email: swllsch@yahoo.com. Phone: +1 815-544-4993.

[Contact source](https://www.jalawn.com/untitled-cnav)

**Qualify first:** Confirm they want more work, can fulfil it, and control the website. Their traffic, margins and willingness to pay are not public.

**Outreach draft — not sent:**

> Jeff, your customer reviews show an established business. Your homepage gives far more space to the logo than to your projects and reputation. I suggest a page that makes the service area, recent work and quote request immediately clear. The aim would be more suitable enquiries from the visitors you already receive. Would a homepage concept be useful?

## 2. Henningson Farms — DeWitt, Michigan

[Henningson Farms](https://www.henningsonfarms.com/)

Recent expansion and established sales channels make this a stronger farm prospect than an unproven hobby business.

**Evidence:** An August 2026 Capital City Market profile reports six full-time staff and supply to Meijer Market Stores. Michigan announced a $93,876 production-expansion grant in March 2025. This establishes investment in growth; it does not establish an available website budget.

- [Retailer profile, August 2026](https://www.capitalcitymarket.com/tastes-blog/henningsonfarms)
- [Michigan expansion announcement](https://www.michigan.gov/mdard/about/media/pressreleases/2025/03/28/mdard-grant-program-announces-newest-recipients)

**Website observations:**

- The opening message is a generic farm welcome. It does not immediately explain location, customer types or the next purchase step.
- Separate DIY, wholesale and dahlia pages already exist. The opportunity is to make these choices and the farm’s business proof much more prominent on the homepage.
- The DIY page puts its three price tiers inside expandable sections. The wholesale page has a substantial qualification form. That form may be intentional; preserve necessary business checks.

**Offer:** Create a homepage built around three clear choices: event buckets, florist supply and dahlia shopping. Add the farm story and retailer proof. Show DIY prices and a useful arrangement example before the enquiry button.

**Measure:** Completed DIY enquiries and paid orders; approved wholesale accounts and repeat orders. Measure each customer group separately.

**Public contact:** Suzi Henningson / Kayla — identified in the retailer profile. Public farm email: henningsonfarms@gmail.com. Phone: +1 517-881-0583.

[Contact source](https://www.henningsonfarms.com/contact)

**Qualify first:** Ask which sales channel has spare capacity and the best profit. Do not sell more demand for crops or dates already fully allocated.

**Outreach draft — not sent:**

> Suzi, I read about your expansion and Meijer supply relationship. Your homepage could tell that story much more clearly. I suggest showing wedding buyers, florists and gardeners their next step immediately. We could start with the homepage and measure enquiries and paid orders by customer type. The Abundant Blooms case is a useful comparison, with results attributed to its designer.

## 3. Impact Pro Refinishing — California — Modesto, Sacramento and San Jose listed

[Impact Pro Refinishing](https://www.impactrefinishing.com/)

A visual home-improvement service with an existing quote process that can be improved.

**Evidence:** The company site describes a family business founded in 2017 and links to before-and-after work. BuildZoom corroborates a Ceres business under the same name, but does not provide customer reviews. Independent evidence of customer satisfaction was weaker than for J&A.

- [Company website](https://www.impactrefinishing.com/)
- [Business corroboration](https://www.buildzoom.com/contractor/impact-pro-refinishing-llc)

**Website observations:**

- A long quote form occupies much of the opening section before visitors see project proof.
- The homepage repeats the quote process in a second form. Both include instructions to refresh and retry if the confirmation page does not appear.
- The opening claim is broad. A service-area statement and a clear cabinet transformation would be more informative. The warning text is not proof that submissions currently fail.

**Offer:** Show a matched before-and-after kitchen first. Explain the finish, service area and process briefly. Use one staged enquiry journey and check its delivery with the owner.

**Measure:** Enquiries completed → estimates attended → profitable projects booked. Compare lead quality as well as quantity.

**Public contact:** Public website email: luis@impactrefinishing.com. Main website phone: +1 877-530-7767. Confirm who handles website decisions.

[Contact source](https://www.impactrefinishing.com/)

**Qualify first:** Confirm current review history, quote delivery, service coverage and existing marketing support. A focused improvement may be easier to sell than a full rebrand.

**Outreach draft — not sent:**

> Your cabinet work is highly visual. I noticed visitors reach a long quote form before seeing much of that work, and another quote form appears later. I suggest showing a clear kitchen transformation first and simplifying the enquiry path. We could measure completed enquiries and booked estimates to see whether the change helps.

## 4. DeMar’s Landscaping LLC — Branford, Connecticut

[DeMar’s Landscaping LLC](https://www.demarslandscapingct.com/)

Established services and a specific opportunity to improve the estimate-request journey.

**Evidence:** The website states operation since 1985. BBB identifies Michael DeMartio as owner and records incorporation in 1995. BBB is business corroboration here; it is not proof of strong customer demand.

- [Company website](https://www.demarslandscapingct.com/)
- [BBB profile](https://www.bbb.org/us/ct/branford/profile/landscape-contractors/demars-landscaping-llc-0111-17006185)

**Website observations:**

- The opening image emphasizes the business premises. Its generic headline does little to explain the result a homeowner can expect.
- At the inspected desktop size, the main estimate button sits below the large opening image.
- That button leads to a Contact page showing telephone, email and hours. No enquiry form was visible in the inspected page. This creates an extra step for visitors who want to request an estimate online.

**Offer:** Use finished landscaping or patio work in the opening section. Make the estimate button visible immediately. Provide a short form with service and town selection, alongside the existing phone option.

**Measure:** Estimate requests by service and town → estimates booked → jobs won.

**Public contact:** Michael DeMartio — owner listed by BBB. Public business email: info@demarslandscapingllc.com. Landscaping phone: +1 203-483-5252.

[Contact source](https://www.demarslandscapingct.com/contact)

**Qualify first:** Confirm website access, any provider agreement and the team’s ability to respond to forms. Recent independent review evidence remains limited.

**Outreach draft — not sent:**

> Your estimate button takes visitors to phone and email details. I suggest giving people a short estimate form right there, with project type and town. A homepage led by completed work could make the offer clearer too. We could track which enquiries become booked estimates and jobs.

## 5. Interlochen Blooms — Interlochen, Michigan

[Interlochen Blooms](https://www.interlochenblooms.com/)

Real flowers, direct online sales and several customer groups; the likely budget still needs careful qualification.

**Evidence:** The website names Erin as founder and states a 2018 start. The Flower Station lists Interlochen Blooms among its local suppliers. The business also claims a 2025 local readers’ award; that award was not independently verified in this review.

- [Company website](https://www.interlochenblooms.com/)
- [Florist partner listing](https://www.suttonsbayflorist.com/faqs)

**Website observations:**

- The large header and opening layout use considerable space before customers reach the offer.
- Delivery, wholesale supply and weddings share a long homepage. Weddings have no dedicated item in the inspected main navigation.
- An online shop already exists. Keep that working foundation and improve how different buyers find the right offer. Search-index prices differed from the live shop, so confirm prices directly before writing a concept.

**Offer:** Give delivery customers, wedding buyers and florists separate, clear entry points. Bring seasonal work and delivery information forward. Add an event enquiry path with date, location and approximate needs.

**Measure:** Paid orders and completed wedding enquiries. Use average order profit to decide whether a redesign can pay back.

**Public contact:** Erin — founder named on the site. Public business email: interlochenblooms@gmail.com. Phone: +1 517-416-1747.

[Contact source](https://www.interlochenblooms.com/#homecontact)

**Qualify first:** Prioritize only if wedding, wholesale or repeat-order demand supports the fee. Small bouquet sales alone may not justify a large redesign.

**Outreach draft — not sent:**

> Your website serves flower-delivery buyers, wedding clients and florists. I suggest making those three choices obvious at the top so people reach the right offer quickly. A small homepage improvement could preserve your existing shop while giving weddings a clearer enquiry path. We would measure actual orders and useful enquiries.
