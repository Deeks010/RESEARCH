# Global competitors: commercial scale and price evidence

Checked 15 September 2026. Currency: US dollars unless stated. **No audited revenue statements were found for these six private companies.** Voxel does publish an annual recurring revenue threshold. Other disclosed figures include growth rates, a Protex forecast and CompScience insurance premium volume. These are different measures.

## What the units mean

- **Customer company:** a corporate account. One account can have many sites. A named deployment does not reveal contract value or whether every location pays.
- **Signed account:** a contract exists. Public customer language rarely supplies contract status, payment status, start date or renewal status. Exact signed-and-paying account counts remain unknown here.
- **Deployed site:** a location using the product. It can be a pilot, temporary construction project or production installation. Counts may be cumulative rather than currently active.
- **Revenue:** recognized sales during a stated period. **ARR** is annualized recurring contract value at a point in time. Forecast ARR is not achieved ARR or revenue.
- **Insurance premium:** money charged for insurance coverage. An intermediary's commissions and fees are only part of that amount. Funding, valuation and customer cost savings are not revenue.

### Protex sources and limits

## Transparent calculations

### A. Real price, hypothetical deployments: Protex list-price illustration

Published 12-month inputs: **$45,000/site + $2,500/camera**. Renewal treatment and discounts need confirmation. [Vendor marketplace table; checked 15 September 2026](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-673or3sh2ujpe)

| Hypothetical footprint | Calculation | 12-month list amount |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 company, 1 site, 5 cameras | 45,000 + 5 × 2,500 | $57,500 |
| 1 company, 1 site, 20 cameras | 45,000 + 20 × 2,500 | $95,000 |
| 1 company, 3 sites, 20 cameras/site | 3 × 95,000 | $285,000 |
| 10 companies, 3 sites each, 20 cameras/site | 10 × 3 × 95,000 | $2,850,000 |

These are **purchase-scope calculations, not Protex revenue estimates**. They demonstrate why companies and sites must remain separate. Hardware/services bundled in a contract also prevent automatically calling the whole amount software ARR.

### B. Missing price: calculate what scale would mean, not a fictitious vendor rate

For a camera subscription: annual recurring contract value = paying companies × active paid sites/company × billed cameras/site × monthly rate × 12. Actual recognized revenue additionally depends on commencement, billing terms, discounts, cancellations and service delivery.

**Hypothetical assumptions only:** 20 billed cameras per site and $50, $100 or $200 per camera-month. These are sensitivity inputs selected for arithmetic, not discovered prices for Intenseye, Voxel, viAct or Avathon.

| Assumed active paid sites | $50/camera-month | $100/camera-month | $200/camera-month |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | $1.2m/year | $2.4m/year | $4.8m/year |
| 300 | $3.6m/year | $7.2m/year | $14.4m/year |
| 500 | $6.0m/year | $12.0m/year | $24.0m/year |

How to interpret by vendor:

- **Intenseye:** enterprise customer count alone cannot select a row. Need paid sites and cameras per account, then the actual rate.
- **Voxel:** reported hundreds of sites fits the order of magnitude, but historical sites and today's ARR are not matched dates. No row is a reconstructed Voxel figure.
- **viAct:** 300 sites is close to its April 2025 reported lower threshold. The $7.2m middle cell holds only if all 300 are concurrently paid for a year at the invented 20-camera/$100 assumptions. Construction closures and hardware/services could change it substantially.
- **Avathon:** no verified comparable installed count selects a row. Applying the matrix to a customer's 20,000-location footprint would be unjustified.

### C. CompScience premium-to-fee sensitivity

At the reported $50m premium-volume scale, an **assumed** retained share of 10%, 15% or 20% produces **$5m, $7.5m or $10m** before expenses. Formula: premium volume × assumed share. These percentages are illustrative, not the company's disclosed commissions. The premium period is unclear, so the results are not labeled annual revenue. Profit-sharing, fees, partners and accounting recognition could alter the result. Factory-vision revenue remains unknowable from this math.

### D. Useful benchmark from Voxel's disclosed threshold

An annual recurring amount of $10m would imply $100,000/site-year at 100 paid sites, $33,333 at 300, or $20,000 at 500. This is **division of a benchmark by hypothetical site counts**, not Voxel's realized site price. It helps test how many paid factories Kenesis would need at a chosen economic model without treating employees as a billing unit.

## Implication for scaling SOP validation — assessment

Large deployment numbers do not prove that arbitrary SOPs work from arbitrary camera views. Scaling a company can mean repeating a narrow safety package, doing paid custom engineering, or supplying cameras and hardware. Commercial claims need the correct product scope.

For Kenesis, the critical economic test is whether the second, third and tenth factory require less setup work. Record configuration hours, new training examples, rejected camera views, ongoing support hours and paid cameras for each rollout. Separate reusable SOP checks from custom work and quote custom work explicitly. Public competitor revenue estimates cannot answer that product-margin question.
