# Can factory SOP monitoring become repeatable software?

Research date: 15 September 2026. India is Kenesis's practical starting market. Current Kenesis deployments are unpaid pilots; paid traction is zero. This supplement investigates delivery effort and commercial evidence for Retrocausal, Invisible AI, Cogniphi, Spot AI and historical Drishti Technologies. No products were tested or vendors contacted. Technical descriptions below use vendors' documentation, vendor-authored cases or direct founder testimony. Financial information is classified by source quality.

## Answer

**The concern is valid: different camera views and different processes create real deployment work. The reviewed companies reduce and package that work; their evidence does not show that arbitrary SOPs work on arbitrary CCTV without projects.**

The strongest public example is Cogniphi's four-month deployment involving 500 legacy cameras and 1,100 sensors. Retrocausal openly describes process-specific labeling/training and custom integrations. Invisible AI supplies dedicated depth-camera equipment and on-site support. Spot AI teaches the system from reference processes and distinguishes general video alerts from its operations product.

A reusable software business is possible if each additional station mainly needs configuration, validation and bounded support. It is not established if every sale needs new perception research or months of integration. No audited gross-margin breakdown was found for these five companies.

## 1. What the specialists actually do

### Retrocausal: reusable onboarding with process-specific learning

Its published Assembly Copilot workflow is: specify the SOP, record a few cycles, label one cycle, train a model and deploy. It claims onboarding within hours and support for webcams, PoE and GigE cameras. Crucially, the same site says its solutions team visits facilities for custom manufacturing-system integration. Customer-success staff also help standardize processes. This is not evidence that all deployment work disappears. [Official homepage](https://retrocausal.ai/)

Kaizen Copilot is a different scope: an engineer records a workstation cycle with a stationary smartphone or webcam, uploads it and gets time-study/ergonomics/process analysis. A single demonstration for analysis is not proof that continuous production monitoring will correctly recognize every variant and exception. [Kaizen product](https://retrocausal.ai/kaizen/)

**Engineering lesson:** keep shared capture, annotation, training, reporting and integration tools. Treat the learned process and its acceptance test as explicit per-process work. The published onboarding claim is vendor-reported; no cross-view benchmark or quantified support cost was found.

### Invisible AI: standardize the sensing equipment and support delivery

Its system uses edge devices containing a RealSense 3D camera, NVIDIA AI chipset and local SSD storage. This differs materially from unrestricted reuse of ordinary CCTV. [How it works](https://www.invisible.ai/how-it-works/)

The current 100-day offer includes up to 20 edge cameras over 10 stations, on-site training, remote support, a continuous-improvement sprint and an ROI assessment. No price is displayed. This is direct evidence that installation, training and operational adoption are part of delivery. [Trial offer](https://www.invisible.ai/one-line-offer/)

The Toyota announcement describes a 2022 initial deployment of 500 devices at Indiana within a partnership covering 14 North American manufacturing operations. It also says the companies collaborated for two years before the milestone. Its “60 seconds” deployment claim therefore must not be presented as the end-to-end duration of qualifying, installing and validating a plant. [May 2022 announcement](https://www.invisible.ai/news/invisible-ai-partners-with-toyota-to-install-ground-breaking-computer-vision-platform-across-north-america/)

**Engineering lesson:** standardized camera hardware and geometry can reduce variability. The customer still needs a suitable view, process context and adoption support. Kenesis can remain a software company while publishing approved camera/view requirements and using installation partners.

### Cogniphi: public evidence of a substantial industrial project

Its tyre-manufacturing case describes existing-CCTV inspection, trained object/action models, SCADA and sensor integration, annotated areas and countdown rules. Camera roles separately monitor operators and components; processing uses a local server with cloud storage. The project reports 500 legacy cameras plus 1,100 active sensors, across 300 acres and over 800 machines, completed within four months. The document also describes two plants; these are not two independent customers. [Official case study, pp.3–9](https://cogniphi.com/wp-content/themes/cognipi/assets/pdf/Cogniphi%20-%20Case%20Study-1.pdf)

**Engineering lesson:** the reusable part appears to be the vision/integration platform. Mapping material types, process events and machine status remains domain work. The report does not reveal headcount, labor hours or margin. It does not prove equal deployment effort for the next customer. Avoid copying its unsupported claim that facial emotions establish worker efficiency.

Cogniphi's broader site explicitly promotes organization-specific patterns and expert implementation. Its Indian tyre-customer testimonial thanks the team for factory visits and continuing support. [Official site](https://cogniphi.com/)

### Spot AI: reusable detection plus taught process context

Spot AI separates general Video AI Agents from AI Operations Assistant for workstation workflows, changeovers and SOP adherence. [Manufacturing offering](https://www.spot.ai/manufacturing)

Its operations description says customers teach a reference process using video, images and SOPs, then evaluate runs with step-level scorecards. It warns against starting with poor visibility or undocumented SOPs and recommends integration with production data. The example is an anonymous packaging manufacturer: five live plants are claimed, with 19 planned by end-2027; the latter is not deployed scale. [Vendor-authored changeover guide](https://www.spot.ai/blog/reduce-changeover-time-video-analytics)

Deployment uses an Intelligent Video Recorder and existing/new cameras. Partners lead deployment in its current channel model; support includes onboarding. [System overview](https://www.spot.ai/), [partner program](https://www.spot.ai/become-a-partner), [support](https://www.spot.ai/support)

**Engineering lesson:** a library and shared platform can reduce repeat work, while partner installation and process teaching still exist. No public source reviewed establishes the labor cost or failure rate of teaching a genuinely novel SOP.

### Drishti Technologies: historical managed service, not assumed current competitor

In direct founder testimony, Prasad Akella described proprietary video/action recognition delivered as a service: Drishti performed the work so customers did not need data scientists. That relocates engineering effort to the vendor; it does not prove the effort is absent. Publicly verified labeling hours or a transferable camera-installation manual were not found. [Founder Q&A, April 2022, preserved on his site](https://akellas.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Drishti-PowerMotion-220505.pdf)

CB Insights reports acquisition by Apple in September 2023; Drishti's European consulting partner says summer 2023. Treat the exact month as unresolved. The partner names historical customers including Flex, Denso, Hella, Toyota, Apple and Bosch, but supplies no complete customer or deployment count. Current independent commercial availability was not established. Do not confuse this company with unrelated products also named Drishti. [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/drishti-technologies), [commercial partner's account](https://www.stm-stieler.de/zh/projects/drishti)

### An actual Spot AI quote and what the arithmetic means

A publicly hosted Coral Academy of Science Las Vegas sales-order proposal dated 16 April 2025 lists 270 AI camera feeds, recorder/platform licenses, 64 new dome cameras, support and a **120-month** term. Subtotal is $1,291,600; savings $681,940; net $609,660 before tax. The document alone does not prove acceptance or payment. [Public proposal](https://app2.boardontrack.com/org/nWT4CI/attachment/publicDownload/392443?s=nWT4CI)

- AI-feed line before discounts: $972,000 ÷ 270 ÷ 120 = **$30/feed-month**.
- Whole discounted package allocated evenly: $609,660 ÷ 270 ÷ 120 = **$18.82/feed-month**.
- Whole package annualized over ten years: **$60,966/year**. Payment terms say due on receipt; this is not an annual billing quote.

The lower blended net figure reflects a large bundle discount and long commitment. Discount allocation between hardware/platform/agents is unspecified. It cannot establish a software-only SKU price, monthly cancellable plan, Indian price or detailed SOP-monitoring price. Public directory claims of $99/month were not verified against an official current rate card and are excluded.

## 3. Redesign Kenesis around what must actually change

The following is a proposed workflow, not a claim that Kenesis has implemented it.

### Three deployment categories

| Category | Example | What changes at the next site | Commercial treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Configure an existing visible rule | Person inside a restricted polygon; station unattended beyond a duration when staffing is expected | Zone, timing, schedule, expected machine state and scene validation | Standard software package with capped onboarding. |
| Teach a new visible activity/sequence | Place a specific component, then perform a distinguishable tool action | Demonstrations, labels, variants, process model and held-out tests; perhaps a different view | Scoped paid process onboarding. Promote to a reusable package only after repeat deployments. |
| Requirement is not visible or not visually distinguishable | Correct torque, internal seal position, chemical concentration, true machine idle cause, conversation content from silent CCTV | Another sensor/system signal or approved camera/view is needed | Integrate an authoritative measurement, change scope or decline. Do not guess and call it compliance. |

**Changing angle does not always require rebuilding the entire application.** It can require zone recalibration, additional examples or retraining the recognition component. Video access, user management, review screens, evidence storage, schedules, alerts and reporting should remain shared. Whether the perception can transfer is an empirical test, not a sales promise.

### Proposed delivery flow

1. **Define one observable checkpoint.** Ask what physical evidence distinguishes pass, fail and unknown. Identify exceptions and the business cost. Avoid starting with an entire written SOP.
2. **Run a camera-readiness gate.** Sample all shifts and variants. Check whether a human reviewer can reliably see the difference from the existing view. If humans cannot see it, model development is not the first fix. Record approved angles, lighting and minimum visible detail for that checkpoint.
3. **Choose configure, teach or integrate.** Keep site rules separate from shared recognition. Link machine/tool state when video alone cannot establish the process state. The configuration is reusable only if the next site does not require a new engineering branch.
4. **Validate on unseen runs.** Reserve later shifts, workers and variants from training. Measure missed events, false alerts, review burden and the share of time reported as unobservable. Test after moving a camera or changing a product. Unknown must stay unknown.
5. **Price and bound the service.** Standard fee for the tested package; separately priced new activities, integration and approved camera repositioning. Record onboarding and monthly support hours. Expand only after a buyer pays and a second independent deployment demonstrates reuse.

For current unpaid pilots, workstation coverage linked to verified avoidable delay remains a bounded candidate. Do not infer that absence causes low productivity or that a group is “chitchatting.” Machine downtime, missing material and legitimate off-camera tasks can explain the same image.

## 4. Calculations that determine whether SOP work scales

Vendor disclosures above anchor the cost categories: trained processes, integrations, devices, installation and ongoing support. They do **not** disclose labor costs. All numbers below are explicit Kenesis planning assumptions, not competitor benchmarks or accepted buyer prices.

Assume ₹6 lakh annual software fee per site, ₹1.2 lakh annual compute/hosting cost, ₹1,500 fully loaded delivery cost/hour and ₹30,000 travel/setup cash cost. Exclude sales, R&D, corporate overhead and tax, so this is contribution before those expenses, not accounting profit.

| Assumed delivery burden | Repeatable package | Bespoke SOP project |
|---|---:|---:|
| Onboarding hours | 40 | 400 |
| Onboarding labor cost | ₹60,000 | ₹6,00,000 |
| Support hours per month | 4 | 20 |
| Annual support cost | ₹72,000 | ₹3,60,000 |
| Annual compute/hosting | ₹1,20,000 | ₹1,20,000 |
| One-time travel/setup cash cost | ₹30,000 | ₹30,000 |
| First-year contribution without setup fee | **₹3,18,000 (53%)** | **−₹5,10,000 (−85%)** |
| Subsequent annual contribution at same workload | **₹4,08,000 (68%)** | **₹1,20,000 (20%)** |

Formula: annual fee − compute − annual support labor − onboarding labor − one-time travel/setup. A setup fee could cover onboarding, but it does not cure expensive recurring support. Replacing each assumed number with measured pilot data is mandatory before a forecast.

Capacity illustrates the same problem: two delivery staff × 120 usable delivery hours/month = 240 hours/month. Before support, 40-hour deployments permit six sites/month; 400-hour projects permit 0.6 sites/month. With 20 live sites at four support hours/month, remaining capacity is 160 hours, enough for four 40-hour deployments. At 20 support hours/site, support alone consumes 400 hours: the two-person team is already over capacity. Utilization and hours are assumptions, not industry norms.

**The scaling gate is therefore measured repeat effort, not camera count or a large feature catalogue.** Track hours per new camera view, per new activity, per integration and per live site-month. If those figures fail to fall across similar deployments, Kenesis is selling an engineering service at software pricing.

## 5. What to ask vendors in a real comparison

Request a quote for the same written scope: ten stations, two camera views each, three product variants, specified checkpoints, all shifts and a factory-system integration. Require separate prices for onboarding, cameras/compute, subscription, new SOPs, camera relocation, support and exit/export.

Ask who labels/trains, how many demonstrations are needed, what happens when a checkpoint is hidden, which integrations are standard, the time from first video to accepted live operation, and what proportion of deployments need new engineering. Ask for a comparable reference and an unseen-video test. Without that information, public “plug-and-play” claims cannot establish comparable delivery economics.
