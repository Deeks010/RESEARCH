# Kenesis Vision: market and competitor assessment

**Decision: first test whether Indian factories will pay to reduce avoidable delays at workstations that need an operator. Treat this as a hypothesis, not a validated business.**

Prepared 15 September 2026. Covers 20 current vendor offerings, one historical company, and major substitutes. **Founder-confirmed stage: unpaid pilots, no paid traction. India is the practical first market.** Requested features: workstation absence, chitchatting, SOP validation, restricted-area entry, a CCTV-data chatbot and fall detection. These are customer requests, not validated buying commitments.

**Start with the [assessment of your six requested features](YOUR_SIX_USE_CASES.md).** It narrows the broad market research to your actual pilot stage.

## Read this first

Factories already adopt video AI. Tata Steel's own reporting confirms factory use. Multiple competitors sell software that connects to existing CCTV. This validates the category, but not Kenesis's pricing, market share or reason to win. [Tata Steel annual report](https://www.tatasteel.com/investors/integrated-report-2022-23/tata-steel-ir-2022-23.pdf)

**My recommendation:** use workstation absence as the first commercial experiment, but measure avoidable operating delays rather than treating absence as proof of lost productivity. Confirm that a staffed station is actually needed, exclude planned breaks and establish who can respond. Camera reuse explains delivery; recovered value must justify payment.

No defensible unoccupied feature emerged. That does not rule out a business. It means the advantage must be demonstrated through buyer preference, deployment effort, support cost and repeatable results.

## 1. What customers buy

The camera is an input. The purchasing reason is an expensive recurring problem that someone can act on.

| Problem | Likely buyer | Useful proof | Common reason a pilot fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vehicle–pedestrian risk | Safety head and plant manager | Fewer verified dangerous exposures; completed corrective actions | Alerts do not change traffic or behavior |
| PPE / restricted entry | Safety and contractor management | Useful alerts with low review burden | Basic camera analytics already sufficient |
| Material delays / visible stoppages | Production manager | Recoverable productive time and usable output | Video cannot distinguish why the machine stopped |
| Counting / dispatch mismatch | Operations or loss prevention | Reconciled counts and verified avoided loss | Occlusion, duplicate counting or no reliable ground truth |
| Assembly sequence errors | Production and quality | Fewer verified omissions/rework | Ceiling cameras cannot see small process details |
| Fine product defects | Quality manager | Valid inspection results at production speed | Requires dedicated imaging and lighting |

These are buyer hypotheses. Validate them against current customer records. Competition and technical limits are documented in the linked research below.

## 4. What pricing tells us

Protex's AWS vendor table lists $45,000 per site and $2,500 per camera for 12 months. One site with 20 cameras totals **$95,000 at that listed scope**. This is not a negotiated quote or proof of subsequent renewal pricing. [AWS listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-673or3sh2ujpe)

Visionify advertises a **$3,000 starter kit** with local computing, up to ten existing camera connections and three months' subscription. It is not a full-year equivalent or perpetual license. [Starter kit](https://www.visionify.ai/starter-kit)

Intenseye describes annual per-camera pricing, with separate modules and hardware quotes. Public numeric enterprise rates were not established. [Pricing](https://www.intenseye.com/pricing)

**Conclusion:** buyers can encounter very different scopes and prices. Do not price Kenesis as a percentage below one headline. Compare the same use case, camera count, analysis hours, computing, support and contract term. Public competitor revenue, market share and comparable renewal rates were not reliably established.

## 5. Market size: useful boundaries, no invented certainty

India's ASI 2023–24 release estimates 260,061 covered factories/establishments. This is a reference population, not a count of customers with useful CCTV and budget. [Government release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2161192&lang=2&reg=3)

No credible public denominator was established for factories that meet all three conditions: the right problem, usable camera coverage and willingness to pay. A generic global video-analytics market number would mix unrelated security, retail, government and hardware spending.

The [market analysis](market_validation.md#2-market-sizing-without-an-inflated-aicctv-total) provides transparent sensitivity calculations. Treat them as arithmetic, not a forecast. For planning, start with a named list of reachable factories and observed pilot conversion.

## 6. Where Kenesis could win

### Recommended first choice: workstation coverage linked to avoidable delays

Use the existing absence-detection request to test one scheduled workstation process. Ask the production manager to verify whether absence causes an avoidable delay and what that delay costs. Do not infer why someone left or whether they were working elsewhere. The factory industry remains unspecified, so choose the first narrowly defined process from the pilot sites.

Test these hypotheses in order:

| Hypothesis | Why it could matter | Evidence against assuming uniqueness | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| One recurring factory loss with a fixed solution and outcome report | Easier for the buyer to justify and for Kenesis to repeat | Cogniphi, Staqu and Assert already pursue process-specific value | Three similar paid deployments; compare support effort and buyer preference |
| Faster, cheaper installation for small camera footprints | Enterprise projects may be uneconomic at small sites | Visionify already offers a small starter package | Compare full first-year and renewal costs at equal scope |
| Reliable operation on the customer's available computing | Can reduce procurement friction | Local/desktop options already exist | Prove performance and support cost on a stated equipment list |

**The moat to build:** repeated wins in one process, reliable deployment, buyer-approved outcome data, and customer relationships. These are proposed advantages to earn, not present assets inferred from your requirement.

A candidate positioning sentence to validate: **“Kenesis Vision helps [specific factory type] reduce [one measured loss] using compatible existing cameras, with [agreed outcome] verified during a paid pilot.”** Do not fill the brackets with unsupported claims.

## 7. Conditions that could break the software model

Reusing cameras does not remove computing, installation or support work. Ordinary cameras may provide streams but cannot run Kenesis's model themselves. Local PCs, third-party devices or cloud processing can still fit a software business.

Some losses are not visible. A stopped machine may look identical to a working one. A ceiling view may not show a missing component. Process signals or a better camera may be necessary. Do not promise compatibility before checking useful visibility.

If every customer needs new engineering, the company is selling projects even if invoices call it software. Track setup hours, model changes, support load and gross profit per site.

Privacy and worker acceptance also affect deployment. Existing security footage is not automatically cleared for every new use. India rules have phased commencement; requirements depend on location and purpose. [Current legal and technical source notes](market_validation.md#6-privacy-and-trust-affect-buying)

## 8. The next validation decision

First ask one existing pilot site to commit to a bounded paid extension with an agreed result and production price. If it declines, record whether the cause is insufficient value, budget, accuracy or deployment burden. After one commitment, seek two comparable sites. Independently review both alerts and footage without alerts. Measure intervention and business results, not just detection counts.

Scale only when the product transfers across factories, buyers convert at an economic price and ongoing support is manageable. A short safety pilot cannot prove injury prevention from zero observed injuries.

The [validation playbook](validation_and_research_playbook.md) contains the customer evidence sheet, interview questions, comparable quote brief, six-week pilot design, proposed gates and economics method. No outreach or product trials were performed during this research.

## Research quality and remaining uncertainty

Three research agents investigated separate areas. The lead researcher checked major claims and examined additional production competitors. The saved profiles preserve sources and contradictions so research does not depend on remembering one long conversation.

Primary product sources establish what suppliers advertise. Buyer-authored reports strengthen adoption evidence. Vendor case studies remain claims; their percentages are not forecasts for Kenesis. Conflicting metrics, historical products and incomplete pricing are flagged rather than silently reconciled.

This is a completed public-source assessment. It is not an exhaustive census of every supplier or completed commercial validation of Kenesis. The remaining decisive evidence is inside your initial deployments and future paid buying decisions.
