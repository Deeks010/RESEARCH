# Factory operations competitors and adjacent products

Research date: 15 September 2026. Public-source research. Product capabilities and customer outcomes below are vendor claims unless another source is identified. A listed capability does not establish its accuracy at a new site. Prices are unknown unless explicitly stated.

## Why these competitors matter

Factory productivity is already served. Kenesis cannot claim an empty market simply by moving beyond safety. The opportunity must be a narrower customer problem with better deployment economics and measured results.

| Company | Competitive relationship | Specific overlap | Deployment reality | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cogniphi | Direct | Production losses, material delays, machine/operator availability, safety | Existing cameras; a published industrial installation also used sensors and local servers | Detailed but anonymous vendor case |
| Spot AI | Direct/broader platform | Safety, congestion, workflow disruption; separate workstation operations product | Existing compatible cameras plus recorder/compute and cloud | Named customer testimonials; detailed capability claims |
| Retrocausal | Adjacent; direct when selling assembly workflow measurement | Assembly step checks, time studies, line balancing, ergonomics | Webcam/industrial-camera and PC options; useful view of work required | Vendor cases plus partner confirmation |
| Invisible AI | Adjacent system competitor | Cycle times, wait states, interruptions and assembly analysis | Purpose-built 3D cameras with built-in AI computing | Named Toyota deployment announcement, vendor-hosted |
| Drishti AI, Kishangarh | Emerging direct niche competitor | Stone-factory machine stoppages and slab tracking | Existing cameras plus local mini PC | Public product description; no named customer outcome verified |
| Drishti Technologies | Historical reference; current independent availability unverified | Manual assembly analysis | Do not treat old product information as a current offer | Investors identify it as acquired |

## Cogniphi

The manufacturing offering covers camera-based factory intelligence, safety, logistics and quality-related workflows. Its implementation sequence includes discovery, solution design, pilot and scale-up. That is evidence of a project-led sales process, not proof of instant installation. [Manufacturing offering](https://cogniphi.com/industry-manufacturing/)

A tyre-manufacturer case describes delays in material/component changeovers. It reports 40% lower losses and 16% greater productivity. These are vendor-published results for an unnamed customer. Their measurement basis is not independently established. Do not infer customer identity from descriptive clues. [Case summary](https://cogniphi.com/case-study/besting-shortfalls-in-production-with-ai-vision-a-case-study/)

The associated document describes 500 legacy cameras, 1,100 sensors, 800-plus machines and local servers, with four months to implementation. This is a useful warning: published gains came from a larger integrated system. Kenesis should not attribute them to CCTV software alone. [Detailed case](https://cogniphi.com/wp-content/themes/cognipi/assets/pdf/Cogniphi%20-%20Case%20Study-1.pdf)

**Implication:** Material-starvation and downtime detection are not unoccupied categories. Benchmark Kenesis on repeatability, small-site cost and intervention outcomes. Public comparable pricing was not verified.

## Spot AI

Its manufacturing page explicitly advertises PPE, restricted-area entry, congestion, unattended workstations and workflow disruptions. It distinguishes real-time video agents from an Operations Assistant focused on workstation workflows, changeovers and procedure adherence. Named testimonials include Staccato, Elite Comfort Solutions and Allied Stone. They indicate customer use; they do not establish audited financial gains. [Manufacturing offering](https://www.spot.ai/manufacturing)

Spot describes a local video recorder with cloud management. Its published commercial model is a per-camera subscription including recorder hardware. Treat its comparisons with other vendors as marketing; the useful evidence here concerns its own offering. [Deployment and subscription description](https://www.spot.ai/compare/vs/spot-ai-vs-traditional-vms)

The inspected pricing URL redirected to its homepage. No binding public numeric rate was established. Avoid using reseller reference rates as an official quote. [Pricing route](https://www.spot.ai/pricing)

**Implication:** Combining safety and operations is already competitive territory. Kenesis needs a workflow advantage beyond a combined dashboard.

## Retrocausal

Assembly Copilot checks process steps and links video to assembly cycles. Kaizen Copilot offers time studies and line balancing. Ergo Copilot covers ergonomics. The website describes Windows-PC deployment and support for many webcams, PoE and GigE cameras. It also notes that small-component workflows depend on useful camera positioning and standardized part locations. This is not proof that arbitrary ceiling CCTV can resolve assembly steps. [Product and setup descriptions](https://retrocausal.ai/)

Siemens confirms a research collaboration and an integration with Process Simulate Human. This establishes partner engagement, not universal compatibility or performance. [Siemens partner record](https://www.siemens.com/en-us/company/siemens-software-for-startups/siemens-dynamo/)

The case library includes a 3M story spanning 15 plants, alongside anonymous manufacturers. Public comparable subscription prices were not verified. [Case library](https://retrocausal.ai/case-studies/)

**Implication:** Time studies and procedure checking have specialized competitors. Compete only where existing footage is sufficient and the customer benefits from continuous measurement.

## Invisible AI

Its production product links cycle time, waiting and interruptions to video. It explicitly uses 3D camera devices with built-in computing. This is an alternative solution to the same buyer problem, rather than a pure existing-CCTV software peer. [Production product](https://www.invisible.ai/production/)

A May 2022 vendor announcement names Toyota Motor North America. It describes an initial 500-device Toyota Indiana deployment and an intended footprint across 14 North American manufacturing operations. The announcement does not prove all 14 were subsequently completed or that the installation remains unchanged today. [Toyota announcement](https://www.invisible.ai/news/invisible-ai-partners-with-toyota-to-install-ground-breaking-computer-vision-platform-across-north-america/)

**Implication:** Existing-camera reuse may lower installation friction. Dedicated views may deliver better measurements. Test both the cost and visibility tradeoff. Public price not verified.

## Two different Drishti identities

**Drishti AI in Kishangarh** advertises local monitoring for stone factories. Its site describes machine run/stop detection, operator presence, safety zones and slab capture. A mini PC connects to existing cameras. The thinwire-analysis feature is marked under development. No named customer or measured commercial outcome was established in the reviewed page. This is an emerging watchlist competitor, not proven scale. [Product site](https://www.getdrishtiai.com/)

**Drishti Technologies**, historically associated with manual assembly analytics, is marked acquired by its investors. Do not confuse it with the Kishangarh product or assume a current standalone sales offering. [Emergence portfolio](https://www.emcap.com/portfolio/drishti), [Alpha Intelligence Capital portfolio](https://aicapital.ai/our-companies/)

## What this evidence rules out

Existing-camera support, operations analytics, local processing and factory specialization are each already advertised. Their combination might still serve a particular buyer better, but that requires customer evidence. Website silence about a feature is not proof a competitor lacks it.
