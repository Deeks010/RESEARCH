# Kenesis Vision: market demand, economics and constraints

Research date: 15 September 2026. Scope: factory AI using existing CCTV. India is the practical initial market, as clarified by the founder. Global evidence is included for comparison. All links were researched on this date. This is desk research; no buyer interviews, customer references, paid quotes or product tests were performed.

**Founder-confirmed stage:** Kenesis has unpaid pilots only and zero paid traction. Requested use cases include absence from workstations, “chitchatting,” SOP validation, restricted entry, a CCTV chatbot and fall detection. These requests establish interest and access to test environments. They do not establish willingness to pay, renewal or repeatable software economics.

## Decision

**Demand for factory video analytics is real. A defensible market for Kenesis specifically is not yet proven.** Buyer-authored industrial reports establish actual use. Existing-camera compatibility, PPE alerts, dashboards, privacy controls and integrations are already offered by competitors and substitutes. Kenesis's next gate is its first paid conversion, followed by repeating a measurable customer outcome across similar factories without repeated custom engineering.

Confidence: high that the category exists; medium that a focused offering can be sold; low on Kenesis's achievable pricing, margins, win rate and unique advantage until its initial deployments are measured.

### Scale of the underlying safety burden

The ILO's 2023 estimates put annual work-related deaths at nearly 3 million, predominantly disease-related, with about 330,000 accident deaths and 395 million non-fatal work injuries. Manufacturing is one of several high-hazard sectors. **Do not imply that CCTV can prevent all these outcomes.** Many diseases, exposures and off-camera hazards cannot be identified through ordinary video. [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/nearly-3-million-people-die-work-related-accidents-and-diseases)

For a manufacturing-specific comparison, US BLS reports 355 manufacturing fatalities in 2024 and 2.7 recordable injury/illness cases per 100 full-time workers. These figures measure a problem, not an AI budget. [BLS manufacturing statistics](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag31-33.htm)

India's DGFASLI Standard Reference Note 2024 reports 2023 registered-factory fatal injury rates of 5.46 per lakh workers. This is administrative registered-factory data, not a full estimate of all Indian workers or informal factories. Reporting coverage and definitions prevent treating it as directly comparable to the ILO or US figures. The report's indexed text was available; full PDF opening failed in this research environment. [DGFASLI report](https://dgfasli.gov.in/public/Admin/Cms/AllPdf/688c8b32d39859.65933706.pdf), [official report directory](https://dgfasli.gov.in/en/Standard-reference-notes)

## 2. Market sizing without an inflated AI/CCTV total

India's ASI 2023–24 release estimates **260,061 factories/covered establishments** and **19,589,131 persons engaged**. Its coverage includes registered factories and certain other establishments. It is not a list of 260,061 viable customers. The live sampling frame is separately 261,818; these figures must not be mixed. Tamil Nadu, Gujarat and Maharashtra account for 15.43%, 12.81% and 10.20% of the factory total respectively. Food, non-metallic minerals, textiles, fabricated metals and rubber/plastics lead by number of factories. [MoSPI/PIB release, Tables 3–4 and coverage notes](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2161192&lang=2&reg=3)

For international scale only, BLS lists **403,603 private manufacturing establishments in Q1 2026, preliminary**. This is not a globally comparable factory total and cannot be added mechanically to India's ASI count. [BLS, establishments table](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag31-33.htm)

**No defensible global count of factories with usable CCTV, an affordable problem and an available AI budget was established.** Generic video-analytics market reports combine security, transport, retail, government, hardware and software. Their total is unsuitable as Kenesis's serviceable market.

### A transparent India sensitivity model — assumptions, not forecast

Use 260,061 only as a starting reference. Apply three sequential filters: relevant segment/plant economics; usable camera coverage of the chosen problem; willingness and ability to buy. The percentages below are deliberately hypothetical and must be replaced with evidence.

| Assumption | Narrow | Working illustration | Broad |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Relevant plant/segment share | 5% | 10% | 20% |
| Usable CCTV share among those plants | 40% | 60% | 75% |
| Commercially ready share of remaining plants | 20% | 30% | 40% |
| Resulting qualified sites, rounded | 1,040 | 4,681 | 15,604 |
| Assumed annual software fee/site | ₹3 lakh | ₹6 lakh | ₹12 lakh |
| Illustrative annual opportunity | ₹31.2 crore | ₹280.9 crore | ₹1,872.4 crore |

Formula: 260,061 × segment share × usable-CCTV share × buyer-readiness share × assumed annual fee. This range is sensitivity arithmetic, **not a validated TAM or forecast**. No claim is made that the stated fees are competitors' prices or accepted by buyers. Hardware, installation and services revenue are excluded. Do not infer Kenesis can win a standard percentage of these sites.

For an achievable revenue plan, use a named account list and capacity: qualified opportunities × pilot participation × paid-production conversion × sites/customer × annual software fee. Example only: 100 qualified opportunities × 30% pilot participation × 50% paid-production conversion × 2 sites × ₹6 lakh = ₹1.8 crore annual recurring revenue once all sites are live. The assumed conversion is not Kenesis's current performance: paid traction is zero. Time-to-live, churn, renewal, expansion and sales staffing remain separate assumptions.

## 3. Buyers and the reasons they may pay

These are working buyer hypotheses, not validated Kenesis customer findings.

| Job to solve | Likely champion / budget owner | Evidence required before selling broadly |
|---|---|---|
| Workstation coverage gaps linked to avoidable production delay | Plant operations + production manager | Whether the station should be staffed at that time; planned breaks/changeovers; machine and material availability; verified output/delay records; action the supervisor can take; willingness to pay for the resulting improvement. |
| Repeated vehicle–pedestrian conflict in internal roads, loading or movement zones | EHS head + plant operations | Exposure frequency, high-risk zones, current controls and whether existing camera geometry can measure the interaction. |
| Restricted-zone entry during a specific process state | EHS + production/engineering | Need for machine-state data; alert response owner; whether certified physical safeguards already address it. |
| PPE exceptions in high-risk areas | EHS + contractor management | Why camera-bundled detection is insufficient; response burden; whether buyers will pay for this alone. |
| Blocked access/exit and recurring obstruction | Plant operations + EHS | Duration, frequency, actionable ownership, acceptable false alarms and existing inspection labor. |
| Bottleneck/queue/process-delay measurement | Operations + continuous improvement | Measured cycle time or lost output; proof that video adds information beyond production systems. |

IT/security controls network access and deployment approval. Procurement reviews commercial terms. Workforce representatives may affect acceptance. An EHS champion without an economic owner can create a successful unpaid demonstration that never becomes a contract.

**Conditional recommendation:** use the existing unpaid pilots to test workstation coverage linked to avoidable delay. Choose a station whose expected staffing and production state can be independently verified. A person outside a camera zone is not evidence of low productivity; breaks, material shortages, maintenance and legitimate off-camera duties must be classified. Count verified coverage gaps and attributable delay, then test whether correcting those gaps improves an agreed operating measure. If the link cannot be demonstrated, do not sell a productivity claim.

“Chitchatting” is not an adequate commercial outcome or reliable visual explanation for a delay. Several people together may be coordinating work. The useful question is whether a defined workflow was delayed and what can be changed. Restricted-entry and fall alerts address separate safety workflows. A CCTV chatbot is an interface whose value depends on reliable retrieval and useful answers; its existence is not market validation.

For SOP validation, distinguish a reusable visible rule—such as entering a marked zone or leaving a station uncovered beyond a configurable duration—from complex assembly-sequence validation. The latter can vary by product, tools, camera view and process state. Before promising scale, show that the next site can configure the same rule rather than requiring a new bespoke model and extensive annotation. These are working product hypotheses; detailed feature feasibility needs pilot evidence.

## 5. Deployment economics: software-only is not compute-free

The software can be sold without manufacturing hardware. Inference still runs somewhere: on a compatible camera, on a customer server, on third-party edge equipment or in the cloud. State who owns, supplies, maintains and pays for that compute.

Connection standards solve only part of installation. ONVIF defines profile-specific interoperability; models must actually conform. It does not certify legal compliance or prove a scene can support a particular detection. ONVIF now recommends Profile T as the successor to Profile S; existing Profile S systems continue operating, but its older authentication deserves attention. [Profiles](https://www.onvif.org/profiles/), [October 2025 transition notice](https://www.onvif.org/pressrelease/onvif-to-end-support-for-profile-s/)

Before quoting each site, record: accessible streams; camera model/firmware; angle and distance; day/night visibility; blur and occlusion; frame rate; codec; network separation; compute capacity; video retention; cybersecurity approval; and expected alert load. Axis's published guidance explicitly requires suitable mounting, scenes and objects. [Axis manual](https://help.axis.com/en-us/axis-object-analytics)

Illustrative bandwidth calculation, not a vendor requirement: 20 continuously uploaded streams at 2 Mb/s each require 40 Mb/s uplink and approximately 12.96 TB over 30 days, excluding overhead. Sending selected clips/events from local inference can lower bandwidth, but savings depend on the actual design and retention requirements.

### What the unit economics must include

Annual software revenue minus compute/cloud/storage, support, model maintenance, third-party licensing, channel commission and attributable service delivery gives a practical contribution view. Separately track onboarding labor and installation travel against setup fees. A nominal software margin can hide a custom-services business.

Suggested proof targets for Kenesis, to negotiate against actual customer needs: repeatable installation measured in days; limited remote tuning after launch; improving cost per monitored zone; renewal without founder-led support; and a contribution margin that remains positive at the price buyers accept. These are business goals, not industry benchmarks.

### ROI must be observable

Use verified labor hours saved × fully loaded hourly cost, plus measured avoided downtime × contribution per hour, plus independently supported reductions in scrap or claims where causality can reasonably be shown. Subtract software, compute, setup, alert-review labor and maintenance. Do not count revenue as profit or count the same benefit twice.

Safety pilots should track confirmed high-risk exposures per operating hour and closure of corrective actions. A short pilot with zero injuries is not proof of accident prevention. Before/after comparisons must account for changes in staffing, workload, camera coverage and reporting. More detected events after deployment can mean better observation, not deteriorating safety.

## 6. Privacy and trust affect buying

**India:** The final DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 with staggered commencement. Rule 1 states that Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 begin on publication; Rule 4 after one year; Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 after eighteen months. Do not claim the complete operational rules are already effective on 15 September 2026. Determine the applicable provisions and legal basis for each deployment with qualified local advice. [Official Gazette, rule 1, English text p.24](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)

**EU:** The EDPB's video guidance addresses lawful basis, necessity, proportionality, disclosure, transparency, retention and security. Its April 2026 summary warns against repurposing security footage for unexpected uses such as employee monitoring. An existing camera installation does not automatically authorize new AI purposes. [Guidelines](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-32019-on-processing-of-personal-data-through-video-devices_en), [2026 summary](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-04/summary_edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en.pdf)

Product recommendations: avoid identity tracking unless essential; provide masking, limited retention, permissions and access logs; document data flows and subprocessors; explain the purpose to workers; and separate safety improvement from unsupported individual productivity scoring. Blurring faces alone does not demonstrate anonymity when context can identify someone. These are design recommendations; jurisdiction-specific legal requirements need separate assessment.

## 7. What internet research cannot validate

Kenesis has no paid traction, so a paid renewal rate or proven commercial margin does not yet exist. Future accepted prices, false-positive burden, camera compatibility, deployment effort and independently verified customer outcomes remain unvalidated here. Public competitor pages rarely disclose comparable pricing or head-to-head performance. Lack of a feature on a website does not prove absence. No claim of a unique Kenesis capability should be made from website comparison alone.

### A concrete 30–45 day validation plan

1. Review every unpaid pilot: exact problem, baseline, budget owner, cameras, deployment/support hours, current usage, verified benefit and what would trigger payment. Record the zero-fee status explicitly. Obtain explicit permission before naming a pilot factory publicly.
2. Interview 12–15 qualified prospects in one selected factory segment and region. Include EHS, operations and IT voices. Ask about the last real incident/workflow problem, current spend, rejected options and who can approve a purchase. Avoid asking only whether they like AI.
3. Time-box the existing unpaid pilots around the same bounded use case. Present a concrete production price and acceptance criteria before adding scope. Test representative shifts and operating conditions. Review both alerted events and independently sampled unalerted footage. For workstation coverage, reconcile events with staffing schedules, machine/material status and production records.
4. Record precision, recall where sampleable, false alerts per camera-hour, alert-review minutes, delay, uptime and actionable-event closure. Report results per use case and scene; a blended accuracy number can hide the weakest conditions.
5. Make the first paid production conversion the immediate willingness-to-pay gate. Require a real payment or paid contract commitment; praise and requests for additional free features do not pass. Broader expansion then requires the same package to work at two or more independent factories with acceptable support cost. If payment or repeatability fails, revise the buyer/use case before expanding the feature list.

No emails or other outreach were sent during this research. Buyer interviews, measured review of the existing unpaid pilots and paid-conversion testing remain future validation work.

## Overall recommendation

Start in India with one unpaid pilot that can test workstation coverage against verified avoidable delay. Pursue it only where that operational connection is measurable and the buyer can act on it. Seek the first paid conversion before treating the feature as a market wedge. Offer a candid camera-readiness assessment and an all-in deployment quote. Treat mixed-camera support, local deployment, workflow integrations and privacy as requirements to demonstrate, not unique advantages established by this research.
